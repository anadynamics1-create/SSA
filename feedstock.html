Suitable Feedstock

What can feed a Bio-CNG plant?

Almost any biodegradable organic waste can produce biogas. Learn about the five main feedstock types, then use the estimator to get an indicative idea of your plant's daily Bio-CNG output.

Open the estimator

Feedstock Types

Five common feedstocks.

Indicative biogas yields per tonne as received. Real yields vary with moisture, freshness and pre-treatment and should be confirmed by a laboratory biomethane potential (BMP) test.

Cattle Dung

From dairy farms, gaushalas and cattle sheds. Very consistent and easy to digest, an ideal base feedstock.

Typical 25–40 m³ biogas / tonne

Agricultural Residue

Paddy straw, crop stalks and other farm residue. High gas potential once shredded and pre-treated.

Typical 150–300 m³ / tonne (dry)

Food Waste

From hotels, markets, canteens and food courts. Energy-rich; needs sorting to remove plastics.

Typical 80–150 m³ / tonne

Industrial Organic Waste

Press mud from sugar mills, food- and dairy-processing waste and similar streams.

Typical 70–110 m³ / tonne

Sewage Sludge

From sewage treatment plants. Low solids, so lower gas per tonne, but available in large volumes.

Typical 10–25 m³ / tonne

Before You Plan

A plant is only as good as its feedstock.

Feedstock is the most important factor in a Bio-CNG project. The plant runs every day, so it needs a steady supply of suitable material all year.

  • Year-round availabilityPlan for seasons: crop residue needs storage; dung and food waste come daily.
  • Collection distanceKeep sources close to control transport cost.
  • Quality & contaminationRemove plastics, sand and metal; avoid toxic or chemical waste.
  • Co-digestionMixing feedstocks often stabilises the process and improves gas yield.
  • Supply agreementsWritten tie-ups with suppliers protect long-term operation.
