What is CNG?

Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) is natural gas, mostly methane, compressed to around 200–250 bar so that enough fuel fits in a vehicle or storage cylinder. Conventional CNG comes from fossil natural-gas fields and is supplied through pipelines to CNG stations.

Because methane burns cleanly, CNG produces far less particulate matter and smoke than diesel and lower carbon monoxide than petrol, and it is typically cheaper to run per kilometre.

What is Bio-CNG (CBG)?

Bio-CNG, also called Compressed Biogas (CBG), is made from organic waste. Biogas from anaerobic digestion is purified and upgraded to at least 90% methane (Indian Standard IS 16087) and compressed just like CNG.

Chemically and in use it is practically the same as CNG: it runs in the same vehicles and moves through the same cylinders, stations and pipelines. The difference is the source: renewable waste instead of fossil gas.

Applications

Where Bio-CNG is used.

Vehicles

Cars, autorickshaws, buses, trucks and CNG tractors at CNG stations.

Industry

Boilers, furnaces and process heating, replacing coal, diesel or LPG.

Commercial kitchens

Hotels, canteens and institutions supplied by cascade or pipeline.

City gas networks

Injected into city gas distribution pipelines for homes and businesses.

Fleet operators

Logistics and bus fleets refuelling from dedicated stations.

Power & heat

Gas engines and gensets for captive power and heat.

Safety

Why CNG is considered a safe fuel.

Like any fuel, CNG must be handled correctly. In India, CNG cylinders, storage and dispensing stations are regulated by the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO).

  • Lighter than airIf released, methane rises and disperses instead of pooling.
  • High ignition temperatureMethane needs around 540 °C to self-ignite, much higher than petrol.
  • Narrow flammability rangeIt burns only at roughly 5–15% concentration in air.
  • Strong, tested cylindersCylinders are built and periodically re-tested to strict standards.

India's Push

Policy is driving demand.

Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas

SATAT

Launched in 2018 to set up compressed biogas plants across India and make CBG available as an automotive and industrial fuel through oil marketing companies.

Government of India

CBG Blending Obligation

A phased obligation to blend compressed biogas into CNG (transport) and domestic piped gas, starting at 1% and rising to 5% over the following years.

Ministry of Jal Shakti

GOBARdhan

Promotes converting cattle dung and organic waste into biogas, CBG and organic manure, with a unified registration portal for plants.
